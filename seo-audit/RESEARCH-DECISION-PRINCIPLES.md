# Research decision principles: Outcome first, not prompt first

**Applies to:** Everything in `seo-audit/`, especially the athlete, filmmaker, service, competitor, trends and keyword reports.  
**Established:** 18 November 2026.  
**Owner's objective:** Make **bogdanpascal.com** easier for the *right* people to discover, understand, trust and hire for work that Bogdan actually offers. Preserve the intended visitor experience.  
**Status:** This is a binding **research standard and decision guide**, not permission to implement changes to the live website.

## Non-negotiable instruction for the next AI / researcher

**Do not follow the user's initial suggested tactic literally if evidence shows it will not achieve the user's underlying goal.**

The user is using research to discover what they do **not** already know. They may suggest "use the most popular keyword", "copy what ranks first", "add every service", "optimize every phrase" or another method as a starting hypothesis, **not as an expert specification**.

You must independently determine the best practical method, check whether the suggestion is sound, challenge it tactfully when not, choose the evidence-backed alternative, **do the work rather than just discussing it**, and clearly explain the trade-offs. The user welcomes useful correction and expert direction.

**Interpret the real intent rather than slavishly implementing the wording:** industry-standard service naming; accurate, discoverable pages; qualified enquiries; reliable rankings/data; best performance and user experience. Research decisions should **not** optimize for the number of keywords, the largest raw traffic estimate, superficial SEO scores, or the number of new landing pages.

## Outcomes in order of importance

1. **Qualified production bookings:** Can a casting director, producer, agency or brand hiring this skill locate and contact Bogdan?
2. **Credible and accurate role positioning:** Does the site describe real capabilities, experience, credits, available services and qualifications without implying unverified accreditation?
3. **Discoverability:** Appropriate page is indexed, understandable and competitive for its own search intent, including relevant low-volume hiring terms.
4. **User experience and performance:** Visitors can use the site on mobile; visual portfolio loads efficiently; reel, images and booking path work.
5. **Measurement:** Verify actual impressions, queries, rankings, clicks and enquiries in Google Search Console/analytics when authorized. Revise assumptions with evidence.

**Never exchange higher-priority outcomes for lower-priority vanity metrics.** Example: 10 searches/month for "parkour performer" with booking intent can be more valuable to a working performer than 18,100 informational searches/month for "parkour".

## Rigorous workflow for every service family

### 1. Reality and industry vocabulary
- Inspect exact existing published page, credits, skills, services, reels, CTAs, equipment and evidence; identify terms already used.
- Read role nomenclature in trustworthy professional bodies, casting/film crew directories and real independent competitors. Distinguish **job title**, **service provided**, **technique**, **audience query** and **adjacent capability**.
- Recommend the **most recognized industry-standard service name** first. Good names are natural, credible and recognisable to the buyer.
- List five or more search variants to support the service, **not** to become five new service pages.

### 2. Buyer and search intent
- Identify the *buyer*, the job being hired for and what a successful enquiry looks like.
- Separate `hire/for hire/booking` queries from `how to/definition/school/tutorial`, job-seeking, gaming, entertainment or unrelated branded queries.
- Different job titles are **not automatically synonyms**: e.g., a specialist parkour coordinator is not necessarily the production's registered **Stunt Coordinator** or health-and-safety lead.
- Do not infer rights to market a regulated activity merely because it appears in competitor menus.

### 3. Volume and rankings
- Use **location-appropriate**, date-stamped monthly keyword-search estimates; clearly identify the provider and geography.
- **Google Ads paid competition is not organic ranking difficulty.** Keep advertiser competition, organic keyword difficulty and SERP quality separate.
- Low-volume keyword `null` is **unknown/insufficient data**, not zero searches.
- Don't add close variants' volumes together without checking grouping/deduplication.
- A keyword with higher demand may have low client fit; prioritise fit as well as popularity.
- Evaluate actual Google search-result pages for **commercial relevance**: true service competitors vs directories, job boards, training pages, social media, news, retail products and unrelated terms. A site ranking well does not prove its copy caused that ranking.
- If live SERP destination URLs, locale, organic positions or paid placements cannot be reliably verified, report them as partial discovery instead of claiming exact top-ten results.

### 4. Trends and seasonality
- Use **2004-to-present (historical context), five years (durable direction), 12 months (seasonality/current annual baseline), three months (recent acceleration)** and month-by-month absolute search-volume estimates when available.
- Compare **like-for-like terms** with unchanged geography, category, surface and time ranges; do not compare raw 0-100 indices across separately normalised Trends graphs.
- Google Trends shows **relative normalised interest**, not monthly search counts. A score of 0 can mean insufficient data for a niche phrase, **not no one searches it**.
- Check ambiguities and media spikes: **"Acrobat" is dominated by Adobe Acrobat searches; "freerunning" can refer to gaming**. These do not automatically reflect increased bookings of physical performers.
- For sparse niche terms, use keyword estimates, industry vocabulary and real SERPs. Do not construct fake rising trends from a handful of Trends points.
- Check calendar seasonality relevant to production bookings, but do not infer causality from one viral spike.

### 5. Decision and implementation
- Rank terms by a transparent **client-fit / industry-recognition / hiring-intent / evidence / attainable competition / sustained demand** model. Include uncertainty and exceptions.
- For each service output: recommended public-facing **service name**, primary search topic, supporting terms, search intent, verified volume (or `unknown`), trend context, industry evidence, suitable target page, and suggested content/proof.
- Keep one distinct existing URL per *actual service intent* where useful. **Do not create a new URL for every synonym** or add filler/near-duplicate content.
- Where ranking data is weak, state that a recommendation is professional judgment and which next test would validate it.
- Distinguish **BACKEND** (technical, speed, semantic markup), **SEARCH** (title/meta/share card) and **VISIBLE** (headlines, descriptions, case studies, CTAs/UX) recommendations; **do not change the live site without approval**.
- Prefer preserving an existing filmic design, adjusting copy and semantics naturally, and delivering visible performance gains without harming grain, colour, image quality or usability.

## If the research contradicts the user's initial idea

1. Explain **what the user actually wants to achieve** in one sentence.
2. State **what the data shows**, with source, geography and date.
3. Describe why the suggested method could be counterproductive.
4. Give the **better action and its expected advantage**.
5. Do not ask for permission to keep researching when it is safe to continue, but **ask before changing public copy/design or deleting live files**.

**Examples of correct course-correction**
- User: "Use 'parkour' everywhere because it has the most searches." Better: keep the word naturally, but feature the exact service `Parkour Athlete / Stunt Performer` and `Parkour Coordination` where relevant; generic parkour searches often seek training/definitions.
- User: "Use the biggest Google Trends spike." Better: inspect whether it is gaming/Adobe/meme-related; choose stable qualified hiring demand.
- User: "Name myself Stunt Coordinator so more people find me." Better: verify the precise professional production role, registration and responsibilities first. Do not blur the distinction with specialist `Parkour Coordinator`.
- User: "Make 100 keyword pages." Better: consolidate synonyms into a few expert pages with real footage, credits, capabilities, location and a hiring CTA.
- User: "Delete hidden files to improve speed." Better: measure which resources are actually transferred and used; don't delete functional equipment fragments or active previews.
- Research AI: "This domain ranks #1, so copy its keywords." Better: examine actual content/search intent and distinguish ranking correlations from demonstrable causes.

## Scope and status tracking

Keep the athlete and filmmaker research in separate documents and a single navigable index. Complete one service family fully, recording primary findings and limitations, before progressing to another. Include a machine-readable dataset, date and market. Preserve work so a fresh ChatGPT conversation can resume without repeating the full research.

Each report must explicitly mark:
- **EVIDENCED**: observed source/data;
- **RECOMMENDED**: professional judgment based on evidence;
- **TO VERIFY**: actual Google indexing, qualified bookings, role/qualification facts or live performance not yet established;
- **NOT APPLIED**: research only.

Avoid false precision, invented rankings/volumes, keyword stuffing, fabricated credentials, and unverified named-client performance claims.
