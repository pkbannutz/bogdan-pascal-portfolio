## 1. Service definition, technical distinction and professional naming evidence

**Parkour (P01):** generally an efficient, direct, fluid negotiation of obstacles and movement through environments.  
**Freerunning (P02):** broadly adjacent practice that often foregrounds expressive, creative movement and may integrate flips, spins, tricking and acrobatics into a continuous line or artistic performance.

The distinction is **about what a director is buying**, not an absolute rule about athletes. A single performer can do both. Some practitioners use the words interchangeably and the exact historical/disciplinary boundary is contested; don't weaponise this for aggressive SEO or claim one practice is universally better.

**External definitional support:**
- Cambridge Dictionary: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/dictionary/english/free-running (freerunning similar to parkour with greater focus on creativity/aesthetics)
- International Parkour Federation: https://internationalparkourfederation.org/ (freerunning emphasis on self-expression, flow and creativity)
- Translation institute's terminology history: https://www.iti.org.uk/resource/from-traceur-to-traducteur.html (distinguishes traversal emphasis from expressive flips/tricks)
- Industry buyer guide: https://movementmanagement.co.uk/blog/freerunning-vs-parkour-casting (an **agency's own self-interested framing** of the two casting briefs, not a binding professional standard)

**Commercially distinct P02 briefs:** `showcase expressive movement`, `flowing acrobatic sequence`, `heroic flip for a brand campaign`, `fashion launch physical performance`, `music-video movement`, `brand content led by body movement`. P01's more grounded traversal and film-action work may be appropriate to some of the same briefs, so let the director specify the exact movement.

**Why Freerunner is an occupational identity, not necessarily a separate crew department:** Examples from individual portfolio Mike Wilson, athlete profile Lawrence Rook and talent agency Movement Management show the title used alongside parkour athlete, performer and stunt (with separate scopes). A creative buyer may require a freerunner while a film stunt department uses a different role title in the final credits.
