## 1. Executive positioning

**Best recognized professional name:** **Movement Director**. Use this, not an invented `physical performance director` synonym.  
**Best buyer-focused explanatory phrasing:** **Movement Direction for Film, TV, Commercials & Live Productions**.  
**Best niche distinction:** **Parkour-led Movement Direction & Physical Storytelling**, if that honestly describes the commissioned scope and portfolio.  
**Best website location:** visibly scoped movement-direction service within the athlete portfolio, with a coordination cross-link and one or two **real case studies explicitly attributing movement direction**. Do **not** automatically create a redundant `/athlete/movement-director/` solely because a keyword is measured.

**What a Movement Director sells:** the overall *physical language* of a character, group, story or piece: body language, posture, character physicality, spatial relationship, movement rhythm and rehearsal interpretation in collaboration with a director. That is **not automatically** the person who performs all the acrobatics, choreographs a fight, choreographs all formal dance, or supervises dangerous stunts.

**Why this is distinct from P03 / P08:** P03 is expert parkour planning/coaching and camera collaboration; P07 can shape all manner of *physical expression* in a story, not only parkour. P08 creates specific planned action movement sequences, may overlap, and will have an independent assessment. P04 is person executing stunts, not necessarily creating movement style.

## 2. Independently verified industry definitions and job boundaries

**Most authoritative professional evidence:**
1. [One Dance UK, Movement Director career profile](https://www.onedanceuk.org/programmes/careers-hub/movement-director) explicitly describes movement director as developing the production's physical style with the director, guiding performer posture/gesture/body language and spatial use; separate from choreographer's movement phrase construction.
2. [Equity: Engaging Choreographers and Movement Directors](https://www.equity.org.uk/advice-and-support/area-of-work/engaging-choreographers-and-movement-directors) distinguishes **Movement Director**, **Movement Choreographer** (often head-of-department for filmed media), **Movement Coach** (individual characterization), associate/assistant roles and co-movement director. It recommends accurate job descriptions, appropriate pre-production budget/credit and scope changes in contracts.
3. [Royal Television Society interview](https://rts.org.uk/article/working-lives-movement-director) describes physical embodiment work for actors across film and television, demonstrating the role is not only a dance-stage profession.
4. [3RINGS Movement Directors](https://www.3-rings.co.uk/divisions/talent/movement-directors) is a movement-direction talent representation site for films, fashion, commercials and music. Strong evidence that it is **booked as an independent creative role**.
5. [Sharia Johnson](https://www.shariajohnson.co.uk/) and [Naomi Weijand](https://www.naomiweijand.com/) use recognizable individual `Movement Director` headings with their credited portfolio work. Another independent creative practitioner, [Mark Conway](https://www.markconway.co.uk/movement-director), demonstrates actor-driven movement direction.
6. [Chima Akenzua parkour director](https://www.chimaakenzua.com/parkourdirector) presents specialist **Parkour Coordinator & Movement Director** together while still separating specialist capacities.

**Important distinction:** `Movement Director` is a real role, but a performer who choreographs their own flip or offers the ability to coach actors cannot automatically claim **production-wide Movement Director** credits. Actual creative ownership and scope must be named accurately (e.g. `movement consultant`, `movement coach`, `movement choreographer`, `associate movement director` when true). Co-credit/assistant terms from Equity reflect different responsibilities, not prestige synonyms.
