## 0. The correct answer: real market, conditional personal service

**Best established professional job title:** **Underwater Camera Operator**.  
**Best creative film equivalent:** **Underwater Cinematographer** (when personally responsible for image and underwater filming).  
**Best narrow future offer:** **Underwater Pool Filming / Pool-Based Underwater Camera Work**, **only after confirming camera/role evidence, available housing and safety arrangements**.  
**Not currently substantiated:** fully trained commercial media SCUBA diver; HSE-approved occupational diving qualification and medical, diving contractor/supervisor, underwater dive unit, offshore/open ocean filming, underwater camera housing suitable to any depth, specialist diving camera lighting/control umbilicals, marine rescue team or pool studio hire. Each is separate professional and technical responsibility.

**Honest distinction:** Being comfortable underwater, underwater freediving or practising pool underwater shooting does not automatically qualify someone to provide scuba-based occupational underwater cinematography on a film set. Nor does having Sony FX3, GoPros or an underwater camera housing automatically qualify diving work. A paid producer should define whether the photographer stays **dry on the pool side**, places camera into water on a pole or waterproof rig, **breath-holds underwater**, or **uses scuba/surface-supplied equipment**; legal/crew/medical expectations can differ materially. Consult the production's competent diving supervisor/contractor and UK HSE guidance for any actual dive work.

**Distinguish F11 from F02/F03 and F04:** `Cinematographer`/DoP defines image authorship; `Underwater Camera Operator` defines actual in-water camera craft; person may be both on a particular production if contracted and competent. **Terminology nuances:** `underwater cameraman` appears in older broadcast directories and some current colloquial sites, but **Underwater Camera Operator** is more exact and inclusive. `Water Camera Operator` may include boats, surf, splash/waterline, swimming pools or underwater scuba depending on portfolio, so do not describe every water shoot as deep underwater filming.

## 7. UK diving law and professional safety: actual sources and boundaries

**UK HSE (official regulatory), checked 18 Nov 2026:**
1. [HSE Media Diving Projects L106](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/books/l106.htm) is an **Approved Code of Practice** covering media underwater work, explicitly naming **camera operators**, photographers, stunt performers, presenters and underwater unit crew within the class of media divers. **Do not claim underwater camera work is exempt because it is creative rather than commercial**.
2. [HSE Diving at Work FAQs](https://www.hse.gov.uk/diving/faqs.htm) explicitly asks whether diving regulations apply in a **swimming pool or tank** and answers **yes, though some requirements may be relaxed in certain situations**. The exact applicability to a particular shallow/surface/breath-hold camera setup must be assessed under the real definition and project conditions; don't offer blanket pool exemption.
3. [HSE Working Diver Qualifications](https://www.hse.gov.uk/diving/carr.htm) says professional divers in Great Britain need an **HSE-approved, work-appropriate diving qualification**, and distinguishes Scuba, surface-supplied and other modes; camera/lighting/unit crew in media are relevant project types. The user has **not** demonstrated such a qualification in the sourced film repo.
4. [HSE Diving at Work FAQs, medical](https://www.hse.gov.uk/diving/faqs.htm) says a professional diver at work needs a current HSE-approved fitness-to-dive medical, subject to the actual relevant dive operation context. **No current medical or qualification inferred** from fitness, stunt performance or casual pool shooting.
5. [Diving at Work Regulations 1997](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/1997/2776/made) governs the legal baseline alongside current amendments and codes. Need a competent professional to classify particular underwater work: film shoot with scuba, breath-hold and surface-operated rig may not carry identical requirements.
6. [HSE film production safety](https://www.hse.gov.uk/entertainment/theatre-tv/film.htm) places workplace safety duties on actual production/participants; coordinated safety and risk assessment not optional just because underwater clip is short.

**Do NOT claim `pool-only diving is unregulated`**. Equally, do not incorrectly insist that every camera pointed into water from the pool side legally requires an HSE media diver certificate. **Distinguish actual diving at work from filming while not personally diving.**

**Other countries:** UK diving qualifications/regime do not automatically apply identically in Romania, Netherlands or any EU destination. Check actual local dive-work/safety laws, producer/venue requirements, lifeguards/rescue, depth/temperature/visibility/electrical risk and insurance separately. No international `certified underwater camera operator` claim without documented credential recognition.
