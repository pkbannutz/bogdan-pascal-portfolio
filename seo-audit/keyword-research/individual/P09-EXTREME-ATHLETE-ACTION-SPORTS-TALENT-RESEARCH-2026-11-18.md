# P09 / 30: Extreme Athlete / Action Sports Talent

**Researched:** 18 November 2026  
**Market:** UK-English athlete casting, sport-brand advertising, filmed branded content and event/creator partnerships; international bookings considered separately from UK geographic keywords.  
**Status:** **INDIVIDUAL RESEARCH COMPLETE. NO PUBLIC SITE EDITS.**  
**Verdict:** **REAL buyer/category language but NOT currently a well-defined separate professional SERVICE offering.** Best as a **supporting casting and commercial-market category** with precise **Parkour & Freerunning Athlete for Brand Campaigns** positioning. Do not lead a primary service heading with generic **Extreme Athlete**.

## 0. Executive finding

**The mistake to avoid:** treating `extreme athlete` or `extreme sports talent` as equivalent to `parkour athlete`, `skateboarder`, `snowboarder`, `BMX rider`, `motocross performer`, `climber` and every outdoor/endurance athlete. Action-sports talent agencies represent *multiple distinct disciplines* and sell **brand, sponsorship, media, licensing, social reach and casting services**. Being a skilled parkour performer does not establish the other sports, brand deliverables or athlete management services.

**Strongest professional label:** **Parkour & Freerunning Athlete**, using `Action Sports Talent` as **buyer-side classification** when producers/brands browse sports talent rosters.

**Suggested buyer-facing line:** **Parkour Athlete for Commercials & Brand Campaigns**; this fits the first-party portfolio's Nike, commercial/live and international brands history, and helps filter generic adventure/hobby traffic.

**Business decision:** do not add a standalone `EXTREME ATHLETE` booking page. Show brand/athlete availability as a **clear audience/format use case under P01/P02**, with actual campaign examples and a short `BOOK AN ATHLETE FOR A CAMPAIGN` route if offered. Label P09 **supporting commercial discovery category**, not a core occupation equivalent to P06 Acrobat or P04 Stunt Performer.

## 5. Trends, historical/durable vs short-term

Archived UK Google web-search panels: [all-time+last 3 months](../athlete-discovery-and-long-trends-2026-11-18.json), [five-year+12-month](../athlete-data-raw-2026-11-18.json). Normalized interest is **within the same term panel and time range**, not comparable in absolute figures across periods.

- **2004-Nov 2026** same panel `parkour, freerunning, stunts, acrobatics, extreme sports`: `extreme sports` **mean ~3** relative points, `parkour ~8` over all time. This is broad genre awareness, not commercial athlete hire demand.
- **Five years:** `extreme sports` **mean ~8** versus `parkour ~56` in same panel. `parkour` is more recognised in this measurement but includes sport/game fan traffic.
- **Last twelve months:** no `extreme sports` isolated equivalent Trends panel. The **Ads monthly `extreme athlete` series** ~10-50 searches gives some source context but is **not** Google Trends data.
- **Last three months:** `extreme sports` **mean ~2** and `parkour ~61` in one broad panel, not comparable directly to 5-year normalized averages.
- **No validated breakout:** There is **no supported evidence** of increasing bookings for individual extreme-sports athletes, of a new 2026 `extreme athlete` trend, or seasonal production spend. Broad subject curiosity can grow without film/brand hire inquiries.

**Bottom line:** highly specific `parkour athlete for brand campaign` demand is a more promising buyer-cluster hypothesis despite absent numeric long-tail values; test actual campaign enquiries and Search Console later.
