## 0. Why F03 deserves independent research even though the job overlaps F02

The original 30-service inventory has both F02 `Director of Photography (DoP)` and F03 `Cinematographer`. They are **different audience vocabularies** with **very different Google query volumes** and often overlapping job duties. The task is to evaluate F03 honestly, not force artificial distinction for extra pages.

**Professional authorities:** [ScreenSkills](https://www.screenskills.com/job-profiles/browse/film-and-tv-drama/technical/director-of-photography-dop/) says `also known as Cinematographer (especially when the DoP operates the camera)`; the [UK National Careers Service](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/job-profiles/director-of-photography) explicitly lists cinematographer as an alternative DoP title; [British Society of Cinematographers](https://bscine.com/) refers to the art and craft of the cinematographer and lists member roles as Director of Photography. This is **professional equivalence for the core image leadership role**, with production-by-production variations in crew scale.

**Nuance:** `Cinematographer` is often attractive to clients wanting an individual image-maker, visual storyteller or smaller production crew. `DoP` more readily signals responsibility for a shoot's whole cinematographic image and lighting decisions. In a one-person production the same person may carry both description and formal role. On a big production a DP and a camera operator have separate duties; **F04 Camera Operator is genuinely different**, not a synonym.

**Result:** **One person, one best canonical F02/F03 hiring landing** `/filmmaker/services/dop/`, supported by a combined role name and project-specific film proof. F03 gets its own research record but **not its own near-identical marketing page** absent a genuinely different task/portfolio structure.
